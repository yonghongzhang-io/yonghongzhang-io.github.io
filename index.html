About

Research Profile

I am a PhD candidate at Universidad Autónoma de Madrid working at the intersection of causal inference, climate and environmental policy, and reliable AI for empirical research.

My work asks how empirical conclusions can remain trustworthy when research workflows increasingly involve AI systems. I study both substantive policy questions — especially carbon markets and climate policy — and the design of execution-grounded agents and benchmarks that make AI-assisted research more auditable, reproducible, and verifiable.

Selected Research

Research Themes & Work in Progress

Do Carbon Markets Deliver Environmental Co-Benefits? Evidence from China's ETS Pilots

Causal evidence on carbon-market effects on CO₂ and conventional air pollution, using difference-in-differences, event-study, and spatial analyses.

Execution-Grounded Causal Inference

Benchmarking whether AI systems can move from causal claims to executable, reproducible empirical workflows rather than producing plausible text alone.

Reliable Evaluation of AI Agents

Studying evaluator validity, tool-use reliability, failure attribution, and robustness under realistic research and data-access conditions.
